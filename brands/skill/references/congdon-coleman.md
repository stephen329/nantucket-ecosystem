## Voice — the named advisor, where the strategy requires one

§4 scopes the requirement, and the scope is narrower than "all Congdon & Coleman communication":

<!-- law: docs/strategy/nantucket-ecosystem-integrated-strategy.md -->
> Anything involving representation, valuation, fiduciary judgment, negotiation, or consequential advice comes from a named person.

Those communications carry an advisor's name, direct contact, and signature: market reports, opinions of value, owner advisory letters, and anything touching a client's money or property decisions.

**Operational and service communication may come from the brand.** A request to update a vacation-rental calendar, a portal invitation, a scheduling or document notice — none involve representation, valuation, fiduciary judgment, negotiation, or consequential advice, and none require a named sender. Requiring one there is not a stricter reading of the strategy; it is a different rule.

When a *new* communication flow is designed, whether it needs a named sender is a question to answer and record in that flow's decision. It is a design-time safeguard, not a precondition on sending.

Register: professional, measured, first-person, personally accountable. The advisor states facts, distinguishes fact from judgment, and puts their name on the judgment.

**Worked examples:**

- ❌ "Congdon & Coleman is pleased to announce that the Nantucket market remains strong!"
- ✅ "The mid-market ($2–4M) saw 14 sales this quarter against 9 last year. In my judgment that reflects returning buyer confidence rather than inventory pressure — happy to walk through what it means for your property. — [Name]"
- ❌ Anonymous blast: "Thinking of selling? Contact our team today!"
- ✅ Named letter to an existing client, referencing their property and situation specifically.
