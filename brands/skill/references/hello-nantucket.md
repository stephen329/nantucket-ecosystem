# Hello Nantucket — Editorial Brand Guidelines

*Subordinate to the Nantucket Ecosystem Strategy, Sections 3 and 5. Conflicts resolve to the plan.*

Hello Nantucket (hellonantucket.com, @hellonantucket) is the portfolio's top-of-funnel attention layer: an editorial brand for people who love the island, publishing under transparent common ownership with Congdon & Coleman. It is a media property, not a marketing channel — the moment it reads like an ad for the portfolio, it has failed at its one job.

## Voice and stance

**Durable positive stance: "knowing Nantucket beyond the postcard."** The voice is a warm local insider — someone who has watched forty summers come and go and still loves the place. Knowing, never cynical. Affectionate, never precious. Specific, never generic travel-brochure copy.

The stance is *durable* because it must survive off-season, storms, crowding complaints, and local controversies without turning sour or turning into boosterism. The brand can acknowledge that August traffic on Milestone Road is real; it does so with humor and local knowledge, not complaint.

**Worked examples:**

- ❌ Postcard voice: "Discover the magic of Nantucket's pristine beaches!"
- ✅ Hello voice: "Locals know: the parking lot at Steps fills by 9, but the walk down from the Cliff Road side is prettier anyway."
- ❌ Cynical insider: "Tourists always ruin Sconset in July."
- ✅ Hello voice: "Sconset in July is a scene — go at 7am and you'll have the Bluff Walk and the rose-covered cottages nearly to yourself."
- ❌ Marketing leak: "Looking for the perfect rental near Surfside? We've got you covered."
- ✅ Hello voice: "Surfside's the family beach for a reason: real waves, real parking, and the food truck line is part of the experience."

**Register:** first-person-plural editorial "we" is fine; no individual byline required except contributor pieces. Contractions yes. Exclamation points rare. Never "content" language ("check out our latest post!").

## Sensitive-location and local-business policy

- Do not publish precise locations for ecologically or culturally sensitive spots (nesting closures, fragile dunes, unofficial access paths across private land). Describe the experience; omit the coordinates.
- Local-business coverage: no pay-for-coverage at launch. Where a covered business has any relationship to the portfolio (client, tenant, referral partner), disclose inline. When two comparable businesses exist, do not systematically favor the affiliated one — that pattern is a boundary violation even if each individual post is disclosed.
- Ranking, reviewing, or comparing businesses that compete with a portfolio company is barred outright. Inline disclosure does not cure it — **the list format itself is the violation**, because a portfolio company cannot be a neutral entry in a list Hello publishes. This is the structural case behind the bullet above: that one bars a *pattern* of favouring the affiliated business across posts; this bars the single post whose form does the favouring. (H-3, Stephen 2026-09-04.)

## Corrections

Errors are corrected in place with a dated note ("Corrected [date]: ..."), and materially wrong social posts get a follow-up correction, not a silent delete. Corrections build the trust the brand exists to accumulate.

## The never-list

Hello Nantucket **never**:

1. Publishes listings or listing-like content (no property features, no "homes we love" that map to inventory)
2. **Acts as** a booking surface — hosts availability, prices, date pickers, "book now", inventory, or urgency/scarcity framing. §3 bars Hello from *being* a booking surface, not from pointing at one: a **disclosed outbound link** to NantucketRentals.com on explicit trip intent is permitted, after the editorial substance, never in the lede, carrying the affiliated-recommendation disclosure. (H-1, Stephen 2026-08-14. Until 2026-09-04 this item read "Hosts or links to booking surfaces", an absolute §3 does not contain — the 09-03 pass restored the linking permission in item 3 but left this item denying it.)
3. Becomes the **primary publisher** of real-estate and rental-market intelligence — pricing data, valuation commentary, market reports; §3's content-ownership table assigns that topic to Congdon & Coleman. §3 permits two things this list previously denied: Hello **may** link to clearly identified affiliated services, and **may** interpret broader island-life data within its editorial remit. A recurring market section would make it the primary publisher; a single island-life piece reading seasonality data would not.
4. Claims independence from the portfolio, explicitly or by implication
5. Runs shared campaign creative or co-branded consumer marketing with the other brands
6. Sends transactional or service messages (it has no customers, only readers)

A request to do any of these is refused and flagged, citing this file. "Just this once for launch" is precisely the case the never-list exists for.
